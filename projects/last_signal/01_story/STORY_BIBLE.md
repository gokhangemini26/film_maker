# Story Bible — Last Signal

Everything here is PROPOSED for G2. Canon ids in brackets are the checkable
commitments; this document explains them. Locked intent and tone canon are
constraints and are not restated as decisions.

## Premise
**DECISION** (`story.premise`). Ren, about 20, has finally found the nerve to
invite his university friend Hana to dinner, where he means to tell her he loves
her. She doesn't pick up, so he has to text, and his phone is on its last few
percent. His old car and a corner shop both fail him at the worst moment, and in
the end only his own hands, turning a hand-crank charger he had shoved aside,
can buy enough power to send the invitation.

- **Want:** get the invitation to her before the phone dies.
- **Obstacle:** every borrowed source of power fails (car, shop).
- **Cost:** small doses of dignity and a lot of hurry; in the end his own
  physical effort and patience.

The sent message is the dinner invitation. The confession belongs to the dinner,
after the film ends (USER_REQUIREMENT, G1 note).

## Theme
**DECISION** (`story.theme`). *How much of yourself will you spend to reach one
person, when every shortcut fails?* The film never answers it in words. Its
answer is an action: the borrowed power fails, his own power works.

## Characters in the story
Names are for production only; they never appear on screen (`tone.wordless`).
The names are an ASSUMPTION the human can replace freely. Designs belong to the
character-designer.

| | Ren (he) | Hana (she) |
|---|---|---|
| Given (USER_REQUIREMENT) | about 20, university student, drives a small old car, has a crush on her | about 20, his university friend, doesn't know how he feels |
| Role | protagonist | the person he is trying to reach |
| Want | send the invitation before the phone dies | none in the film; she is the destination |
| Need | to stop looking for a shortcut and put his own effort in | none on screen |
| Arc | nervous and rushing → calm and spent; turns at beat 5 when he chooses the crank (`story.arc.ren`) | a single moment of noticing: phone lights, headphones down, reads, small smile (`story.arc.hana`) |
| Played through | posture, staging, silhouette: hunched over the phone, sprinting, a slump, sitting on the kerb, leaning back | one gesture (headphones sliding down) and one close-up smile |

**Why Hana missed the call (DECISION, `story.arc.hana`):** she has big
headphones on while she sketches at her desk. The audience learns this only at
the end, when the phone lights and she slides them down. It answers "was she
ignoring him?" with a prop, so her silence at the start never reads as
rejection.

No other characters are needed (`story.cast`). If the world-designer puts a
shopkeeper in the shop, it is a background silhouette with no interaction:
nobody helps him, so the solution stays in his hands.

## Where it happens
**DECISION** (`story.locations`). Two spaces for him plus hers, one fewer than
the direction's ceiling:
1. **A quiet street** at golden hour sliding into dusk, his small old car parked
   at the kerb (interior and the kerb beside it).
2. **A small corner shop** a few steps along the same street.
3. **Hana's room**, at her desk by a window, in the evening.

Only the shop loses power, not the street: bad timing, not disaster. The
light moving from golden hour into dusk over the minute gives the direction's
"brighter comic middle, warmer and dimmer tender end" as real time passing. The
country, architecture and signage (shapes only, no words) are the
world-designer's; the story needs none of them to be specific (UNKNOWN).

## Stakes
**DECISION** (`story.escalation`). One meter: the battery percentage. It drops
one step per setback and the crank buys back exactly one.

| Moment | Battery | What it costs him |
|---|---|---|
| The call | 5% | a rehearsed breath, then silence |
| Starts typing | 4%, turns red | the easy option (a call) is gone |
| Car dies | 3% | his own car, his small private hope |
| Shop goes dark | 2% | a public dash, and the whole room goes dark around him |
| The turn | 1%, blinking | nothing left to borrow |
| The crank | 1% → 2% | his own effort, slowly |
| Send → dark | 2% → 0 | he lets it go |

Emotionally the stake is simple and personal: tonight he found the nerve; if the
phone dies, the nerve may not come back. The film never says this; his care
before the call (mirror check, rehearsed breath) shows it.

**Picture cues** (`story.setup_cues`), all wordless:
- *Crush:* mirror hair check, a rehearsed breath, her photo (no name) on the
  call screen.
- *Dinner:* a map pin with a fork-and-knife icon (no labels) on his phone, and
  the invitation itself.
- *What he will say at dinner:* during the crank he types a heart emoji at the
  end of the invitation, hesitates, and deletes it before sending. The audience
  watches him keep it for the dinner.

**The plant** (`story.crank_plant`). The hand-crank charger is Ren's own and
lives in his glovebox. In the car beat he opens the glovebox for his cable, the
chunky crank charger clunks onto his lap, and he shoves it back as useless.
After the shop goes dark he walks back to the car, sits on the kerb, looks at
the glovebox, and takes it out. Rejecting it first is what makes the payoff a
choice rather than luck (`intent.earned_last_signal`); introducing it as a
small gag and then treating it with sincerity is the turn
(`tone.the_turn`).

## Ending and final image
**DECISION** (`story.ending`). The invitation sends: the progress completes, a
tick appears, and he lets go of the crank. Hana's phone lights on her desk; she
slides her headphones down, reads, and gives a small smile. Back on the kerb his
screen fades to black. He doesn't panic.

**Final image:** Ren leaning back against his car on the kerb, the dark phone
resting on his chest, looking up at the dusk sky.

Why it ends on him: the effort was his, and his loose posture with a dead phone
says "it's out of my hands now, and that's fine", which is hope rather than loss
(`tone.ending_restraint`). Because the audience saw him delete the heart, her
smile answers the invitation while the confession is still ahead: open and
hopeful together (`intent.open_hopeful_ending`). Rejected: ending on her smile
(the dark phone becomes an afterthought or a punchline) and ending on the black
screen (reads as a sad cut-off).

## What the story deliberately leaves out
- **Any voice or readable word** except the invitation (`tone.wordless`): no
  voicemail, no "Delivered", no names, no signs, no keyboard letters (see below).
- **The dinner, the confession and any reply** (`tone.ending_restraint`).
- **Her side of the missed call** at the start. We don't cut to her until the
  end, so the audience worries with him and is reassured with her.
- **A second meter.** No signal bars, no clock racing; "last signal" means the
  last of the battery (G1 decision, unchanged).
- **Travel.** No driving sequence, no journey to her; the car never moves.
- **Help from anyone.** No shopkeeper, passer-by or friend hands him the
  solution.
- **Gags after the turn** (`tone.the_turn`).
- **Backstory.** How they met, how long he's liked her: the mirror check and the
  breath are all the history the film needs.

## Notes for later departments (RECOMMENDATION / DEPENDENCY)
- **Phone keyboard letters (from G1 review, item 2).** He types in beats 2 and 6.
  Under strict wordless the keyboard's letter keys must not read: frame the
  keyboard out, blur it, or draw unlettered keys. Owner: look-director and
  cinematographer (G4/G5). The typed invitation itself is the one allowed text.
- **Map pin.** The fork-and-knife pin must have no street names or place labels.
  Owner: look-director (phone UI).
- **Car interior.** Dashboard, radio and charger socket carry no legible text;
  the car dying is sound, a slump and the dash lights going out, no smoke
  (CREATIVE_DIRECTION production constraints). Owner: world-designer.
- **The crank charger** is a hero prop: it must read instantly as "turn this for
  power" from its shape (a fold-out handle), since no label may say so. Owner:
  world-designer / character props.
- **One hand cranks, one thumb types** in beat 6. Animation-director to confirm
  the pose is readable on a proxy rig; fallback is alternating (crank, pause,
  type).
- **Title card and credits** (G1 review, item 1) were not decided in canon;
  that is a creative-director/human matter and does not change the story.
- **Invitation wording** is the screenwriter's job: 2–3 short options (≤12
  words, no confession) for the user at G2 (`story.message`).
