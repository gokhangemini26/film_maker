# Cinematography Bible: Last Signal

PROPOSED for G5 (together with the storyboard and shots). Canon lives in
`canon/camera.yaml` (`camera.*`) and `canon/continuity.yaml` (`continuity.*`).
This document gives the reasoning and the working tables. Positions are in the
street set frame of `world.sets.street`: +Y west (sun, shop), +X north (across
the road), Z up, metres. Statements are tagged FACT / DECISION / ASSUMPTION /
RECOMMENDATION / DEPENDENCY / UNKNOWN.

## 1. Camera philosophy
- **DECISION: in the comedy the camera is a patient, affectionate observer; after
  the turn it is a companion at his level.** Up to the shop blackout the camera
  stays still, at eye level or wider, and lets the world's bad timing land inside
  a held frame. It never mocks him. From the kerb on, it comes down to his seated
  height, closes in, and moves only twice, both times toward him, when his effort
  changes something. After the send it stops moving altogether.
  *Why:* `tone.comedy_source` (timing, not pratfalls) needs a still frame for
  the timing to be seen; `tone.the_turn` (quiet and slow) needs a camera that
  changes its behaviour once, for good. *Rejected:* one consistent camera
  personality (the turn would have to be carried by colour and sound alone).
- **DECISION: the phone is the second character and has its own grammar.** Its
  shots are flat, frontal, still and sized to be read, the way a dialogue
  close-up is sized to be heard (`tone.wordless`, CREATIVE_DIRECTION originality
  statement). See section 8.
- **DECISION: anime layout thinking, cinema lighting logic.** Each frame is built
  as layers (foreground props and hands, the character, the painted set, the sky)
  with depth from value and aerial tint, not from optical blur; held frames with
  bursts of action; follow-pans across painted backgrounds; cut-in inserts
  (`intent.anime_feel`). Within that, every frame has a motivated light direction
  and a lens chosen for what it does to space (`intent.soft_but_cinematic`).
- **DECISION: the camera helps the three weakest points found at the gates.**
  The jacket against the dusk sky (G4 Q4), the legibility of the tick, pin and
  numbers (G2 finding 10, G4 finding 4) and the repeated gag (G2 finding 2, G3
  finding 7) are answered by framing rules in this bible, not by asking for
  changes to locked canon.

## 2. Format (`camera.format`)
| Item | Value | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Aspect ratio | 16:9 (1.778) | DECISION (confirms the brief's `assumed`) |
| Resolution | 1920 x 1080 | DECISION (matches the look canon's line-width maths) |
| Frame rate | 24 fps | DECISION (confirms the brief; all look timings stay valid) |
| Sensor | 36 x 20.25 mm, fit HORIZONTAL, on every camera | DECISION |
| Shortest shot | 12 frames; SC02 cuts 13-20 frames by exception | DECISION |

*Why 16:9, not scope:* the phone is tall, the only text in the film is on it,
and the look's legibility rule is a fraction of frame height. 2.39:1 would take
a quarter of that height away. A seated figure and a girl at a desk also fill
16:9 without dead sides. *Rejected:* 2.39:1 for a "cinematic" signal.

**Frame-height coverage** (FACT, from the sensor): vertical frame size at the
subject = distance x 20.25 / focal length.

| Lens | Horizontal FOV | Vertical FOV | Frame height per metre of distance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 mm | 73.7° | 45.7° | 0.844 m |
| 35 mm | 54.4° | 32.3° | 0.579 m |
| 50 mm | 39.6° | 22.9° | 0.405 m |
| 85 mm | 23.9° | 13.6° | 0.238 m |

## 3. Lens set (`camera.lens_set`, `camera.lens.roles`)
**DECISION: four primes, 24 / 35 / 50 / 85 mm. The comic section works on 24/35
(+85 inserts); the tender section on 50/85 (+ one 35 bookend).** No zooms. Any
other focal length needs a `style_break`.

| mm | Effect | Used for | Never for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **24** | Wide, deep; lines converge hard; slight edge stretch | Car interior mediums (the only lens that fits a medium in a 1.48 m cabin); geometric comic wides; the low look up at the shop ceiling panels | Tender faces (distortion); phone inserts |
| **35** | Wide-normal; keeps the room around him | The street and the dash; the shop master and mediums; the SC04/SC06 westward bookend | Phone inserts; tender close-ups |
| **50** | Normal, no distortion; the camera at his distance | Tender mediums and close-ups; Hana; the final image; the SC01 frontal through the windscreen | Comic wides |
| **85** | Compression, flat graphic planes, background falls away | Every phone and crank insert; tender close-ups | Comic wides; establishing |

*Why:* wide lenses let the ordered world (dashboard, street lines, shelves)
press on him in the gags, which is where `look.style.composition` puts the
comedy; switching to 50/85 at the turn flattens the world behind him, so the
film feels closer and quieter without a single move (`tone.the_turn`). 85 mm
keeps the phone square and readable. *Rejected:* a single lens for the whole
film (no turn) and 24 mm comic close-ups (rubbery faces, `tone.anti_goals`).
*Cost:* the 35 mm bookend is the one wide-ish frame after the turn; it is
justified by the before/after comparison it carries (section 10).

## 4. Camera heights (`camera.height.*`)
| Height | Definition | Means | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Eye level** (default) | lens within 0.1 m of the subject's eye | "we are with him, not judging" | All comic reactions (deadpan); tender close-ups; Hana |
| **High** | lens ≥ 0.5 m above his eye, tilt down ≥ 10° | "the world beats him" | Only SC01 slump onto the wheel (mild) and SC03 blackout master (strongest). Never after the turn |
| **Low, mild** | 0.15-0.3 m below his eye, tilt up ≤ 8° | a quick comic decision | His resets: sitting up to text (SC01), bursting out of the car (SC02) |
| **Low, strong** | lens 0.45-0.6 m above the pavement, tilt up ≈ 12° | "he makes up his mind" | Once: SC04, as he sits back with the crank held in both hands |

Reference eye heights (FACT, `characters.*.proportions`): Ren in the car seat
1.20 m; standing on the pavement 1.71 m; kneeling in the shop 1.05 m; seated
on the kerb about 0.95 m (ASSUMPTION derived from crown 1.06 m minus about
0.11 m); Hana at her desk 1.14 m.

*Why:* the creative direction asks for "low angles when he makes up his mind,
high or wide angles when the world beats him". Spending each sparingly, and
escalating both (mild then strong), turns height into a readable scale: the
shop is the car failure made bigger; the crank is his comic resets made real.
*Rejected:* high angles on every setback (flattens the escalation); the strong
low angle on the look to the glovebox (that is the hesitation, not the choice).

## 5. Movement grammar (`camera.movement.*`)
- **Static (default).** Locked off. The characters and the light move. Every
  shot that moves names its motivation in `rationale.movement`.
- **Pan-follow (comic section).** From a fixed pivot, only while he moves
  across frame; starts just after he starts, stops as he stops. Motivation: his
  movement. Used for the exit, the dash, the drop to his knees.
- **Push-in (SC04 only, at most two, ≤ 0.15 m/s).** (1) The choice: as he looks
  through the open door at the glovebox. (2) The effort: as he starts cranking and the
  glow stops flickering. Motivation: an emotional shift caused by his own effort.
- **After the send: nothing moves.** SC05 and SC06 are locked. The stillness is
  "it's out of his hands now" (`story.ending`).
- **Never:** handheld or shake (not even on the shop's clunk: the gag is that
  nothing moves), zooms, whip pans, orbits, crane or tilt up to the sky,
  flyovers, any move inside a phone insert.

*Rejected:* a gentle constant drift (the usual CG "cinematic" default), which
smears held poses and competes with the phone's tiny changes; a crane to the
sky at the end (sky-led, `look.style.originality_guards`).

### 6.3 The jacket never meets the upper dusk sky (`camera.framing.jacket_off_sky`)

- *Rejected:* relying on the outline and afterglow rim alone (G4 showed it fails
  at the zenith); a darker jacket (reopens locked character canon).

### 6.4 Sky and wires (`camera.framing.sky_and_wires`, L12)

  railing end. **DECISION:** the kerb's dusk sky is therefore shown in one setup

- Wires: at most three thin spans in a frame; never across a face, the phone or
  the crank; never converging on his head. In westward frames the vanishing
  point sits ≥ 15 % of frame width from his head. At most one span in her window.
- *Why:* G3 finding 9 and the originality guards, turned into things a frame can
  fail. *Rejected:* a sky cutaway after the send; hiding the kerb sky entirely
  (her window would have nothing to match, L13).

## 7. Depth and focus (`camera.dof.*`)
- **DECISION: depth of field off (everything sharp) in every shot except phone
  and crank inserts. No focus pulls in the film.** Depth comes from staging at
  least three layers separated by value and aerial tint (L23). The eye is moved
  by light events (the blackout, the flicker steadying, her phone waking) and by
  cuts, not by racking focus.
- **Inserts: f/5.6-f/8** on the 36 mm sensor, focused on the screen plane (or
  the pip), lens within 15° of perpendicular to the screen. At 85 mm and 0.3 m
  that holds roughly 1-2 cm sharp: the whole screen and a thumb above it; the
  lap or desk behind falls away. No emissive other than the phone or pip in the
  defocused area.
- *Why:* painted backgrounds are already soft; optical blur on cel outlines
  smears them, and defocused lamps and windows form bokeh discs, which
  `look.style.glow` forbids. *Rejected:* shallow-focus tender close-ups, which at
  dusk would put bokeh on the lit windows behind him.
- ASSUMPTION: Blender 5.2 EEVEE camera DOF behaves as in 4.x. Verify at M3.

## 8. Inserts language: the phone and the crank (`camera.inserts.*`)
**DECISION: 85 mm (50 mm when hands and both objects must share the frame),
locked, from the owner's side (never upside down), square to the screen within
15°.** Two insert sizes:

|---|---|---|

| **Text insert** | ≤ UI cap height ÷ 0.035 (about half the screen) | The invitation, the heart, a battery change, the send and the tick |

**Legibility minimums (DECISION, `camera.inserts.legibility`):**
| Element | Minimum size (fraction of frame height) | Hold |
|---|---|---|

## 9. Phone-lit framing (`camera.phone_lit.framing`)
Applies from the SC03 blackout, through SC04, to SC05 after her phone wakes and
SC06 until the screen is off.
- The lens is on the lit side: within 45° of the direction the screen faces.
- The phone, or its glowing edge, is in frame in the **first shot of every
  phone-lit setup**, so the light is seen to come from it.
- The screen is the brightest thing in the head-and-hands area (L9); no other
  practical within 20 % of frame width of his face.
- **SC06 fade:** the screen itself is in frame, facing out, so the fade is read
  on the screen even if the constant colour ramp flips his face from lit to
  shadow in one frame (G4 finding 5 is the look-director's to solve; this keeps
  the beat safe either way).
- *Rejected:* phone-lit faces from the dark side for mood (rim-only faces lose
  the expressions the tender section needs).

## 10. Screen direction and per-scene geography (`camera.geography.*`)
**DECISION: west is screen left for the whole film, and west is where he is
trying to get to.** The sun and windscreen in the car, the shop down the
pavement, the socket, the glovebox and crank at the kerb, the afterglow he
looks up at, and Hana's window are all on screen left. His effort and his look
travel right to left. Lateral shots keep this; axis shots (straight along the
street, the aisle or the car) are neutral bridges and never reverse it.
*Why:* one compass for three spaces in 60 s; right-to-left runs against the
reading direction, which suits a man the world keeps pushing back; and it puts
the camera on the pavement side, where the kerb, the open door and the ending
are. The world's left-hand traffic makes the kerb the passenger side
(`world.setting`, `world.locations.ren_car`). *Rejected:* left-to-right
"progress", which needs the camera on the road side, with the car between the
lens and the kerb.

## 11. How the two gags avoid repeating (`camera.gags.no_repeat`)
G2 finding 2 and G3 finding 7: the shop runs beat for beat like the car. The
camera tells the same joke bigger and later:

| | Car (SC01) | Shop (SC03) |
|---|---|---|
| Scale | Close, inside, 24/50 mm | The room: high wide master, 35 mm |
| Hope shown | Insert: bolt beside 4 % | Over his shoulder, phone large in the foreground (no insert) |
| Anticipation | none: relief comes at once | 24 mm low angle past him up at the ceiling panels: he checks, **nothing happens** |
| Loss shown | Insert: bolt gone, 3 %, the 3-frame dip | The master, unchanged: every light off in one frame, only his small lit face left |
| Order | insert → reaction → insert → reaction | reaction (exhale) → room → insert (2 %) → reaction |
| Reaction | Close, frontal, forehead slowly to the wheel (≈ 1.7 s) | Reverse on his phone-lit face, door rectangle behind, the longest comic hold (≥ 2 s) |

*Why:* the audience now expects the pattern, so the shop plays on it: the camera
looks where he fears the failure will come from, nothing happens, and it lands
only after he relaxes, room-sized. *Rejected:* varying only the bolt animation or
the sound, which keeps the same sequence of shots. The "wary relief" pose is the
animation-director's to time (G3 finding 7).

## 15. Feasibility in Blender (MVP)

  horizontal, optional DOF with f-stop and a focus object. Moves are keyframed
  location or rotation only (pans and two straight pushes). FACT: nothing here
  needs a rig beyond an empty to aim at.

  camera visibility (ASSUMPTION for Blender 5.2 EEVEE; verify at M3).

  run on the M3 camera-read gate before any rendering.
