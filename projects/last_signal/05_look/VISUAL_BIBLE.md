# Visual Bible: Last Signal

PROPOSED for G4. When approved, the **Style lock** section below is binding on
every later shot, build and render. Canon lives in `canon/look.yaml`
(`look.style.*`, `look.color.*`, `look.lighting.*`, `look.exposure.*`). Colour
values are in the COLOR_BIBLE and light in the LIGHTING_BIBLE. This document
covers the whole identity and the rules.

## Visual identity in one paragraph
*Last Signal* looks like a hand-painted evening that the audience watches through a
phone. The people and the things they touch are flat cel shapes with soft coloured
lines. The street, the shop and the room behind them are gently painted pastel
backgrounds with no lines. The film's light is small and handheld: in the golden-hour
comedy the world lights him, and from the moment the shop goes dark the key light is
his own phone and, at the crank, a small amber pip he makes with his hand. Colour is
kept low and soft so two small things can shout: the battery's red, the only red in
the film, and the amber of power flowing. It fails twice and works the third time. The
sky runs from pale gold to a lavender dusk and is the same sky over his kerb and in her
window. It is never the spectacle.

## Realism and stylisation (`look.style.realism`)
- **DECISION: about 3 on a 1 (flat graphic) to 10 (photoreal) scale.** Flat cel
  characters and hero props with coloured outlines over softly painted backgrounds.
  Nothing tries to look photographic.
- **Why proxies look intentional here (DECISION):** the characters are simple
  primitives (`characters.*.representation`). Flat two-tone fills, a hard terminator
  and a coloured outline turn primitives into graphic shapes. The eye reads them as
  design, the way it reads a cel drawing. Under PBR shading the same primitives look
  unfinished. Detail is spent where the canon spends it: drawn toon faces, hands, the
  phone screen, the crank. Bodies stay sculptural and smooth.
- **Characters and hero props** (`look.style.character_shading`): two hard tones.
  The lit tone is the base mixed 30 % toward (base x key colour), so warm light warms
  without muddying. Measured on the jacket under the golden sun: full multiply gives
  grey-green `#7C857B`; the 30 % mix keeps it blue, `#7C95AE`. The shadow tone is
  base x the scene's shadow tint. One rim tone only when a motivated source is behind.
  Specular only as a painted eye catchlight.
- **Backgrounds** (`look.style.background_shading`): a soft three-step ramp with a
  feathered terminator, faint paper/brush texture (5 % value variation or less), no
  outlines. Beyond about 20 m, flat colour with one light/shadow split.
- *Rejected:* semi-real 3D (exposes the proxies, cannot hold pastels); fully flat
  unlit graphics (soft but not cinematic).

## Texture (`look.style.texture_and_grain`)
- Surfaces are matte. Kind wear (sun fade, scuffs, the jacket's faded cuffs) is
  painted as one or two flat tone patches, never bump or noise.
- One uniform monochrome grain at 1.5 % luminance over every frame, inserts included.
  At that level it cannot affect the invitation's legibility.
- Optional vignette of 10 % or less, identical on every shot. No chromatic aberration,
  no sharpening, no grading LUT on top of the canon palette.

## Atmosphere (`look.style.atmosphere`, `look.style.glow`)
- **Clear, dry, calm air** (`world.rules.weather`): no fog, haze volumes, rain, dust
  motes, floating particles, god rays or lens flares.
- **Depth from aerial perspective only.** Distant elements are tinted toward the
  scene's horizon colour: 0 % at 10 m, 35 % at 60 m, 60 % on the silhouette cards
  beyond the west railing (mist pass mixed in the compositor).
- **Glow** only around emissives (phone screens, amber indicators, fascia sign,
  fridges, lit windows, street lamps, desk lamp): a soft halo no wider than 1.5 % of
  frame width, no streaks, stars or ghosts, no bokeh discs.

## Composition rules (`look.style.composition`)
Rules for the cinematographer (G5), each checkable on a frame:
1. **Comic section (SC01-SC03):** geometric, ground-heavy frames. The horizon or
   rooftop line sits in the middle or upper third. Symmetric one-point frames (the
   street's vanishing point, the shop aisle) are allowed only here, for "the world
   beats him" beats.
2. **Tender section (SC04-SC06):** off-centre, closer frames with lead space in his
   look direction. Horizon in the lower third. No symmetric frames.
3. **Depth:** every exterior and shop frame has at least three depth layers separated
   by value and aerial tint, not by depth of field alone.
4. **The phone wins near his face:** from the SC03 blackout on, the phone screen is the
   brightest element inside the head-and-hands area of any frame it is in (extends
   ART_DIRECTION_BIBLE principle 2).
5. **Sky share:** sky fills at most two thirds of any frame, and a frame with sky
   always holds a character or hero prop (`look.style.sky`).
6. **Minimum size:** keep Ren at 15 % of frame height or more where his state matters
   (CHARACTER_BIBLE RECOMMENDATION, confirmed here as a look rule for readability of
   the cel silhouette).

## The phone screen (`look.style.phone_ui`)
The phone is the second character (CREATIVE_DIRECTION originality statement) and the
only place text can appear, so its look is locked in detail.
- **Light-mode UI** in warm off-white (`#F6F2EC`) with dark ink (`#2B303B`). A dark UI
  would not put enough light on faces for the glow motif.
- **Status bar:** the battery icon and its percentage, right-aligned, and **nothing
  else**: no clock, carrier, signal bars, wifi or other icons. This closes G2/G3
  residual 6 and keeps the battery the only meter (`story.escalation`).
- **Battery:** charcoal at 5 %. Red `#E5483B` icon fill and number from 4 %. At 1 % the
  icon blinks (1 Hz, 50 % duty) while the number stays readable. The charging bolt,
  amber, sits left of the icon.
- **Call screen (SC01):** her circular photo (a cel render of Hana, no name), a green
  round call button with a handset glyph that pulses with each ring, then turns grey
  when the call goes unanswered. No label.
- **Map (SC01):** unlabelled pastel blocks and roads; one blue pin (`#4A6FA5`) with a
  white fork-and-knife glyph. No street names.
- **Keyboard:** blank keys. No letters, numbers or symbols on any key. The only glyphs
  are the emoji-face key and the backspace arrow. A pressed key darkens. This holds
  even if a shot must show the keyboard, and it closes the G2 keyboard residual.
- **The invitation:** dark ink on a pale blue sent bubble (`#D3E0F2`), contrast 9.9:1
  (computed). In inserts the cap height is at least 3.5 % of frame height. It is the
  only readable text in the film.
- **The heart (SC04):** a red heart pictogram, typed and deleted. This is the one
  proposed exception to the red rule and is a human question (see COLOR_BIBLE).
- **Send:** an arrow pictogram. After the tap, a thin amber progress line fills in about
  1 s and a single amber tick appears. No "Delivered".
- **Screen light:** one 3-frame dip to 70 % in the frame charging is lost (SC01, SC03).
  An irregular flicker (dips to 70 % for 2-3 frames) at 1 % until the first crank turn,
  then steady. In SC06 a 24-frame ease-out fade to off (`#1F2029`).
- **Hana's phone:** the same UI system. It lights with Ren's circular photo, no name.
  If her screen is shown, it carries only the same invitation text.
- **Assets:** the icon set is drawn in-house. The UI font licence is **UNKNOWN until
  verified**. RECOMMENDATION: an SIL Open Font Licence humanist sans, such as Noto Sans
  or Inter (their OFL status is an ASSUMPTION to check at M3).

## Originality (`look.style.originality_guards`, G3 finding 9)
G3 flagged that the world's combination (an everyday Japanese-style townscape, wires
across a golden sky, two young people linked by phone under one sky) is strongly
associated with one well-known director's films. The world canon is locked and
keeps the townscape and the shared sky. The look therefore keeps the vocabulary and
changes the treatment:

| Taken as a principle | Not taken (our choice instead) |
|---|---|
| Everyday townscape vocabulary (low houses, poles, wires, a corner shop) | Hyper-detailed, luminous backgrounds → **pastel, low-detail painted backgrounds** (sky saturation 0.40 or less) |
| One sky linking two people | Sky cutaways, wire-silhouette transitions, cross-cut skies → **colour match only, one matched pair** (her window / his kerb), and no shot where sky or wires are the subject |
| Evening light as emotion | Lens flares, bloom washes, light particles, sparkles, bokeh → **none**; glow limited to emissives at 1.5 % of frame width |
| Golden hour into dusk | Saturated magic-hour skies → **pale gold, apricot and lavender** |
| | The film's own light identity: **a phone glow and an amber pip in his hands**, not light from the sky |

The comedy staging (geometric, ground-heavy frames with held poses) also has no
counterpart in that body of work. The characters' designs remain checked against
well-known anime leads at G6, as the CHARACTER_BIBLE asks. The blue jacket plus a tuft
is common and should be watched once proxies exist.

## How to break the style deliberately (`look.style.style_break_protocol`)
- A shot may break a rule only by declaring `style_break: {rule, reason, serves}` in
  its shot spec, reviewed at G5, at most once per sequence (SQ01, SQ02).
- **Never breakable:** L6 (only red), L8 (phone UI text), L12 (sky and originality).
  Breaking them would undo `tone.wordless`, the one meter, or G3's originality finding.
- **Pre-approved breaks: none.** The approved screenplay needs none.
- Example of a legitimate break: a symmetric frame in the tender section (L23) if the
  cinematographer argues that a centred final image serves
  `intent.open_hopeful_ending` better, with the reason written in the shot.

## Feasibility (MVP pipeline)
- Everything above is achievable in Blender EEVEE with primitives: toon materials
  (Shader to RGB into a constant ramp), inverted-hull outlines, a gradient world or sky
  dome plus flat cloud cards, emissive planes for screens and windows, a mist-pass tint
  and a glare node in the compositor (`look.style.render_constraints`).
- **ASSUMPTION:** Blender 5.2 keeps the Standard view transform, Shader to RGB in
  EEVEE, Grease Pencil Line Art and the compositor Glare node as in 4.x. Verify at M3
  (`fm doctor` / the builder).
- **Gaps flagged:** none that block the style. The riskiest items are the legibility of
  the invitation at 1080p (thresholds in L8, check at G6) and the proxy face reading in
  the phone's under-light (G6 face test, already planned by the CHARACTER_BIBLE).

## Dependencies and open items
- **G3 finding 1 (Hana's room angle) is unresolved in canon.** L13 needs the window in
  her frame. The lighting is written relative to her, so it works for either fix
  (LIGHTING_BIBLE §SC05). DEPENDENCY on the world-designer and human.
- **Aspect ratio and resolution** belong to the cinematographer. Line widths and
  thresholds here are fractions of frame height so they survive that choice.
