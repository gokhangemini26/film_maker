# Originality

References (films, photographers, painters, games, places) tell us what the
user responds to. They are evidence of taste, never a spec to copy.

## Use references to infer
- lighting characteristics (motivation, contrast ratio, colour temperature split)
- camera language (lens range, movement habits, shot duration, framing)
- production design principles (density, materials, scale, era signals)
- colour relationships (dominant/accent, saturation strategy)
- pacing and emotional register
- composition tendencies (symmetry, negative space, depth staging)

Write each inference as a **principle**, then design an original expression of
it for this film's world and story.

## Never
- reproduce a specific shot, frame, poster, logo, costume, or character design;
- name or visually imitate a copyrighted character, mascot or franchise asset;
- use a real living person's likeness;
- quote screenplay, lyrics or book passages;
- borrow a film's signature (e.g. its specific title typography or iconic set).

## Originality statement
CREATIVE_DIRECTION.md must include a short section:
- which references were given;
- the principles taken from each;
- what this film does that the references do not (its own visual identity).

The qa-supervisor checks later documents against this statement.

## Assets
Do not assume an external asset (model, texture, HDRI, font, music) is usable.
Record source and licence as UNKNOWN until verified; unknown licences are
blocked from final delivery.
